## 第 1 页 · Figma 帧 1 — ANTCHITECTURE

**EN**  Hi everyone, we are Natural Artificial: this is Jief, this is Shania, and I’m Hank. Our project, ANTCHITECTURE, explores how we might experience our surroundings from an ant’s perspective.

**中文**  大家好，我们是 Natural Artificial，由我们三个人组成：这位是 Jief，这位是 Shania，我是 Hank。我们的项目 ANTCHITECTURE，探索如何从蚂蚁的视角重新体验身边的环境。

## 第 2 页 · Figma 帧 2 — What if every place on Earth…

**EN**  Our project starts with a question: what if any place on Earth could be experienced through an ant’s perspective? We want to explore how a change in scale could change the way we experience familiar places.

**中文**  我们的项目从一个问题开始：如果地球上的任何地方，都能通过蚂蚁的视角来体验，会发生什么？我们想探索，当观看世界的尺度改变时，熟悉的环境会带来怎样不同的感受。

## 第 3 页 · Figma 帧 3 — The Project

**EN**  We describe this project as “a canyon world, grown from the tunnels of real ant nests.” It develops from an exhibition installation into a tabletop product, and then into a first-person virtual world.

**中文**  我们把这个项目描述为“一个从真实蚁巢隧道中生长出来的峡谷世界”。它从展厅中的实体装置发展为桌面产品，并进一步延伸到第一人称虚拟世界。

## 第 4 页 · Figma 帧 4 — (image)

**EN**  We began with an interactive installation for an exhibition. A 3D-printed structure, over two metres tall, stood in the middle of a floor projection, with ants living inside. Visitors took part by walking around it. We chose light as a link between people and ants because research shows that ants can sense different parts of the light spectrum. We hoped that changing the light could gently influence their activity.

**中文**  首先，我们介绍中期的互动装置。它由一个两米多高的 3D 打印结构和周围的地面投影组成，蚂蚁生活在结构内部，参观者通过行走参与互动。我们选择灯光作为人与蚂蚁之间的联系，是因为相关研究表明，蚂蚁能够感知不同波段的光。因此，我们希望通过改变光照，以一种温和、间接的方式影响蚂蚁的活动。

## 第 5 页 · Figma 帧 5 — (image)

**EN**  Each hexagon on the floor matched a module on the installation. A camera tracked where people stood, and both the floor tile and its matching module changed colour. We wanted these light changes to influence the ants’ activity. We also planned to photograph their tunnels and project the paths back onto the floor, but we could not complete this feedback loop.

**中文**  地面的每个六边形网格，都对应装置上的一个模块。摄像头识别人的位置后，地面网格和对应模块会一起改变颜色，我们希望通过这些光照变化影响蚂蚁的活动。原本，我们还计划拍摄蚂蚁挖出的路径，再将它们投影回地面，但这一反馈环节没有完整实现。

## 第 6 页 · Figma 帧 6 — Why we kept going

**EN**  The main problem was timing. Ants needed time to dig, while people expected an immediate response. This broke the feedback loop in the exhibition. We developed three connected directions. First, a personal tabletop product for observing ants at home. Second, a first-person world that users can edit and explore. Third, a cloud network where they can explore spaces created by other people nearby. Together, these extend the experience from a short visit into several days of watching, creating, and exploring.

**中文**  主要问题是，蚂蚁活动和挖掘的节奏，与人期待即时反馈的交互节奏不同。这让展厅里的反馈过程出现了断裂。因此，我们发展出三个相互连接的方向：首先，将装置转为家中的桌面个人产品，让人持续观察；其次，将蚁道转化为可以自行编辑和探索的第一人称世界；最后，通过云端连接这些世界，让人以第一人称探索周边其他人创造的空间。这样，整个体验就可以从一次短暂互动，延续为数天的观察、创作与探索。

## 第 7 页 · Figma 帧 7 — A nest, assembled

**EN**  The product has three types of modules. Structure modules connect the units and provide passages for ants. Supply modules have small lids for adding ants, food, or water. Light modules hold the LEDs and have no ant passages. The modules slide together from front to back through trapezoid joints. We assemble the structure flat, add the back acrylic panel and soil, then close the front panel. We stand it upright and introduce ants through the top supply module. They then dig their own paths. After seven days of observation, the ants are collected and released. Their paths are photographed and later uploaded to the cloud.

**中文**  产品由三种模块组成：结构模块负责连接，并提供蚂蚁可以穿行的内部通道；供养模块有可以打开的小盖，用于放入蚂蚁或补充食物和水；发光模块容纳灯珠，蚂蚁不能穿过。模块通过梯形接口前后滑动咬合。使用时，先平放拼好结构，铺上背面亚克力板和沙土，再盖上正面亚克力板。之后将装置立起，从顶部供养模块放入蚂蚁，让它们自行挖掘。七天观察结束后，蚂蚁被收集并放回自然，留下的路径则被拍摄，之后上传到云端。

## 第 8 页 · Figma 帧 8 — Light, connected

**EN**  The LEDs sit under the covers of the light modules. Wires come out through the side openings, leaving the connectors outside. After closing the covers, we connect the plugs by hand along the outer edges, linking them to the Arduino and power supply in the base. We plan to use addressable LEDs, so modules can share one colour or show different colours. The video shows our current control test with ordinary LEDs.

**中文**  灯珠放在发光模块的盖板下面，电线从两侧开口伸出，接头留在外面。盖好盖板后，沿装置外缘手动连接各个接头，并接到底座里的 Arduino 和电源。我们计划使用可寻址 LED，让所有模块一起变色，或者分别显示不同颜色。这里的视频展示的是现有普通 LED 的控制实验，为后续灯光系统提供技术基础。

## 第 9 页 · Figma 帧 9 — Many forms, shared rules

**EN**  These six examples show different ways to combine one, two, or three hexagonal units, alongside the product on a desk. The layout gives us three ways to gently influence the paths. Structure modules provide fixed crossing points between units. Sugar in the supply modules creates food targets that may draw ants towards them. Light modules change the local lighting, which we hope will affect how active the ants are. Together, these conditions may influence the traces left behind, while the ants still dig the detailed paths themselves.

**中文**  这里展示了单个、两个和三个六边形单元的六种组合方式，以及桌面上的使用效果。通过组合，人可以从三个方面温和地影响路径生成：结构模块提供可以穿行的固定连接位置；供养模块中的糖分形成食物目标点，可能吸引蚂蚁向这些位置移动；发光模块改变局部光照，我们希望借此影响蚂蚁的活跃程度。这三个因素共同构成路径形成的条件，而具体的分支和形态仍由蚂蚁自行挖掘产生。

## 第 10 页 · Figma 帧 10 — From the nest to the game

**EN**  This page shows how the game picks up exactly where the installation stopped. The first three steps happen in the installation: the ants move in, people change the light, and over several days tunnels appear in the soil. Then we photograph those tunnels, and our Path Recognize tool traces them into centrelines and junctions. Each network becomes a hexagonal canyon, and in Unity you crawl through it as one ant among the colony. So the ants decide the shape of the world, and people decide what goes into it.

**中文**  这一页说明游戏如何接上装置停下的地方。前三步发生在装置里：蚂蚁入住模块，人群改变灯光，几天后土里出现隧道。之后我们拍下隧道，用自己做的 Path Recognize 工具把它识别成中心线和交汇点。每一张路径网络变成一个六边形峡谷，在 Unity 里你以一只蚂蚁的身份在蚁群中爬行。所以，世界的形状由蚂蚁决定，里面放什么由人决定。

## 第 11 页 · Figma 帧 11 — Two starting points

**EN**  Two things decide what this world looks like. The look comes from Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind — a fungal underworld that glows, a forest alive below ground. We use it as visual reference and we credit it on the slide. The shape comes from our own colony: we photograph the nest through the acrylic, mask the tunnels, and rebuild them in three dimensions. So every corridor you are about to see is one the ants actually dug — none of it is a designer’s idea of a nest. Next: what we grew inside those tunnels.

**中文**  视觉来自《风之谷》——发光的菌类地下世界，一片在地面之下活着的森林。它是参考，我们在页面上注明出处。形状则来自我们自己的蚁群：隔着亚克力拍摄蚁巢，把隧道抠出来，再重建成三维。所以接下来你看到的每一条通道，都是蚂蚁真正挖出来的——没有一处是设计师想象出来的蚁巢。下一页：我们在这些隧道里种了什么。

## 第 12 页 · Figma 帧 12 — A garden inside the nest

**EN**  Inside the tunnels we grow plants. Six shape generators, twenty presets, all written in our own shape lab — every plant is built from points, the same material as the scan, so nothing is modelled by hand and nothing is downloaded. We scatter them along the corridors, denser where a tunnel opens out. On the right is ant view, before anything is placed: a glowing tube lined with plants, light falling from the roof, and the visitor’s tray waiting at the bottom. At this point the nest is a place — but it is our place, not yours. The next slide is what fixes that.

**中文**  在隧道里我们种下植物。六个形态生成器、二十种预设，全部写在我们自己的 shape lab 里——每一株植物都由点构成，与扫描同一种材料，所以没有任何一个是手工建模或下载来的。它们沿通道散布，在隧道变宽的地方更密。右边是蚂蚁视角，还没有放入任何东西：一条发光的管道，内壁长满植物，光从顶部落下，底部是等待放置物件的托盘。到这一步，蚁巢已经是一个地方——但它是我们的地方，不是你的。下一页解决的正是这一点。

## 第 13 页 · Figma 帧 13 — Bring your own things

**EN**  Then we let people bring their own things in. These ten objects come from our own rooms — a record, a pair of boots, an armchair, a jar of flowers. Each is photographed from a few angles and turned into a gaussian splat, so an object dropped into a tunnel is made of the same points as the plants around it and sits in the world rather than on top of it. On the right, God view: the objects floating along the corridor, with the owner’s dock on the bottom edge. This is the moment the nest stops being our model and becomes somebody’s place — which is what makes sharing it, on the next slide, worth anything.

**中文**  接下来，我们让人把自己的东西带进来。这十件物品来自我们自己的房间——一张黑胶唱片、一双靴子、一把扇形沙发、一盆花。每一件都从几个角度拍摄，再转成高斯泼溅，所以被放进隧道的物件与周围的植物由同样的点构成，是长在这个世界里，而不是贴在它上面。右边是上帝视角：物件沿着通道漂浮，底部是主人的物件栏。就是在这一刻，蚁巢不再是我们的模型，而成为了某个人的地方——也正是这一点，让下一页的共享变得有意义。

## 第 14 页 · Figma 帧 14 — The whole flow

**EN**  Here is the whole flow in one diagram. Two inputs build a personal garden: the spatial basis from the ants — nest, tunnels, photo, path recognition — and content from people — their photos, stories and the 3D objects they place. The garden then joins a shared network, where you choose what others may find. From there you enter someone else's garden, explore as an ant, find a story, and bring a credited copy home. That copy feeds back into your own garden and closes the loop. The current build covers entering, exploring and finding; uploading and bringing copies home are next.

**中文**  这张图是完整的流程。两种输入共同搭建个人花园：一是来自蚂蚁的空间基础——蚁巢、隧道、拍照、路径识别；二是来自人的内容——照片、故事和自己摆放的 3D 物件。花园随后加入共享网络，由你决定别人能看到什么。接着你进入别人的花园，以蚂蚁的身份探索，发现故事，并把一份署名副本带回家。这份副本回到你的花园，回路就闭合了。目前的版本已经实现进入、探索和发现；上传与带回副本是下一步。

## 第 16 页 · Figma 帧 16 — Inside the game

**EN**  Inside the game, one round of play has six steps: arrive in a nest, crawl and meet other ants, read a story, cross a light gate, pick a place on the map, and bring something home. **中文**  游戏里的一轮体验有六步：抵达蚁穴、爬行并与其他蚂蚁相遇、阅读故事、穿过光门、在地图上选择去处、把东西带回家。每一步下面的斜体字是设计理由，大多关于玩家在哪里可以选择、在哪里不能选择——比如你可以决定去哪里，但不能决定会遇到什么。最后一步目前还在计划中。

## 第 22 页 · Figma 帧 22 — User Agency under Limited Control

**EN**  My thesis is called User Agency under Limited Control. It starts from a familiar situation: on platforms we can create content, but we rarely see how the system organises discovery and shapes outcomes. Within the Natural–Artificial theme, I ask how design can distribute decisions between users and systems when outcomes can't be fully controlled, and how to keep the influences of users, algorithms and natural processes visible. I compare three stages of our project — the installation, an early virtual garden and the personal-garden proposal — and split the design into three parts: input from ant traces, expression through personal materials, and indirect exploration. The main finding is that agency depends on where choices happen, what they change and how clear the feedback is.

**中文**  我的论文题目是《有限控制下的用户能动性》。出发点是一个熟悉的状况：在平台上我们可以创造内容，却很少看到系统如何组织发现、影响结果。在“自然—人工”的主题下，我研究的是：当结果无法完全控制时，设计如何在用户和系统之间分配决定权，同时让用户、算法和自然过程各自的影响保持可见。我比较了项目的三个阶段——装置、早期虚拟花园和个人花园方案，并把设计拆成三部分：来自蚂蚁痕迹的输入、通过个人素材的表达，以及间接探索。主要结论是：能动性取决于选择发生在哪里、改变了什么，以及反馈是否清楚。

## 第 23 页 · Figma 帧 23 — Who decides, when outcomes can't be fully controlled?

**EN**  The background is platform logic. Users can upload content, but they don't decide how it is ranked, connected or discovered. That raises a question beyond usability: can people take part in setting the conditions through which an experience takes shape? The project uses living ants because their activity is a process the designer doesn't program. My research question has two sides: first, conditions versus rules — what users can set, what rules the system follows, and who decides those rules; second, making participation visible. As a method I use annotated portfolios, reading every image with three questions: what can users decide, what is the system responsible for, and how do natural traces influence the outcome.

**中文**  研究背景是平台逻辑：用户可以上传内容，却不能决定内容如何被排序、连接和发现。这就引出一个超出可用性的问题：人能否参与设定体验形成的条件？项目选择活体蚂蚁，是因为它们的活动是设计者无法编程的过程。我的研究问题有两面：一是条件与规则的区别——用户能设定什么、系统遵循什么规则、规则由谁决定；二是如何让参与变得可见。方法上我采用注释作品集，对每张图提出三个问题：用户能决定什么、系统负责什么、自然痕迹如何影响结果。

## 第 24 页 · Figma 帧 24 — Four lenses, one main one

**EN**  The theory uses four lenses with one main one. Enactive cognition says we understand through action and feedback, so agency is not getting the result you wanted, but being able to influence a process and adjust. Participatory sense-making adds that meaning is made between people, which is why contributors' context must be kept. Actor-network theory helps trace how non-human elements — ants, recognition rules, interfaces — translate and change outcomes. Platform logic and serendipity remind us that systems are never neutral, and that random content is not the same as meaningful discovery. Together they shift the focus to how people encounter and respond to a process they don't fully determine.

**中文**  理论部分用了四个视角，以其中一个为主。具身认知认为我们通过行动和反馈来理解世界，所以能动性不是得到想要的结果，而是能影响过程并做出调整。参与式意义建构补充了意义是在人与人之间形成的，因此必须保留贡献者的语境。行动者网络理论帮助追踪非人要素——蚂蚁、识别规则、界面——如何转译并改变结果。平台逻辑与偶遇研究提醒我们：系统从来不是中立的，随机内容也不等于有意义的发现。这些理论把关注点转向：人如何遭遇并回应一个自己不能完全决定的过程。

## 第 25 页 · Figma 帧 25 — Reference projects

**EN**  These five cases are not about ants, but they all deal with how much a person or a system decides. Hybrid Webs shows that setting conditions doesn't mean drawing the result. r/place and Everyone Draw show that small local actions can build a shared space, but also that shared spaces need clear boundaries. Travel Frog shaped the thesis version: prepare, let the character go, and wait for it to return. Elden Ring shows stories tied to places. From the cases I took three directions: make the influence of natural traces traceable, keep clear boundaries between personal and public content, and keep a recognisable link between conditions and feedback.

**中文**  这五个案例与蚂蚁无关，但都在讨论人或系统能决定多少。Hybrid Webs 说明设定条件并不等于画出结果。r/place 和 Everyone Draw 说明局部的小行动可以构成共享空间，但共享空间也需要清楚的边界。旅行青蛙影响了论文中的版本：做好准备、让角色出发、等待它回来。艾尔登法环展示了与地点绑定的故事。我从这些案例中得出三个方向：让自然痕迹的影响可追溯，在个人内容与公共空间之间保持清楚边界，并在条件设定与反馈之间保持可辨认的联系。

## 第 26 页 · Figma 帧 26 — From theory to design questions

**EN**  Before the design iterations, I turned the theory and cases into a few working distinctions. First, influencing outcomes is not the same as determining rules: ant traces shape the layout, and users can choose parameters, but the rules are still set by people. Second, uncertainty is not automatically meaningful; if users can only wait passively, it becomes a barrier. Third, agency is read through where choices occur, what they affect and how clear the feedback is. Below you can see who holds what in Ant Garden: the ants, the users, the system and the designers each control a different part.

**中文**  在进入设计迭代之前，我把理论和案例整理成几组可以操作的区分。第一，影响结果不等于决定规则：蚂蚁痕迹塑造布局，用户可以选择参数，但规则仍由人设定。第二，不确定性并不天然有意义；如果用户只能被动等待，它反而会成为障碍。第三，能动性要从选择发生在哪里、影响了什么、反馈是否清楚来理解。下方展示了在 Ant Garden 中谁掌握什么：蚂蚁、用户、系统和设计者各自控制不同的部分。

## 第 27 页 · Figma 帧 27 — How the design changed

**EN**  The design went through six versions, and each one solved something while opening a new question. The installation let people change conditions rather than ant routes, but it stayed in one place and time. Whole-space AI was visually rich but couldn't explain why a path became a scene. Path Garden made cause and effect clearer, but the plants were only decoration. Separating paths from personal content gave objects a source, but gardens were still isolated. Gaussian splats added richness but needed context. The Unity game now links gardens and places stories in the world; uploading and bringing copies home are next.

**中文**  设计经历了六个版本，每一版都解决了一个问题，同时打开了新的问题。装置让人改变的是条件而不是蚂蚁路线，但它局限在一个地点和时段。整体 AI 生成视觉丰富，却解释不了为什么一条路径会变成这个场景。Path Garden 让因果更清楚，但植物只是装饰。把路径和个人内容分开，让物件有了来源，但花园之间仍然孤立。高斯泼溅增加了丰富度，却需要语境支撑。现在的 Unity 游戏把花园连起来，并把故事放进世界里；上传生成物件和带回副本是下一步。

## 第 28 页 · Figma 帧 28 — What we found

**EN**  To conclude: limited control can be a starting point for agency, as long as people can tell what they decide, what they only influence, and what requires their response. The change across the project is not more or less control, but where choices sit and how far they reach — users edit their own gardens directly and discover others' content indirectly. The system should act as a rule-bound mediator that shows where it steps in. Next, we want to complete the full loop in the game and look closely at whether people can tell what their choices influenced and what the system changed.

**中文**  总结一下：有限控制可以成为能动性的起点，前提是人能分清哪些由自己决定、哪些只能影响、哪些需要自己回应。整个项目的变化不在于控制更多或更少，而在于选择的位置和影响范围——用户直接编辑自己的花园，间接发现别人的内容。系统应当是一个遵循规则的中介，并让人看到它在哪里介入。下一步，我们希望在游戏里完成整个回路，并进一步观察人们能否分辨自己的选择影响了什么、系统改变了什么。

## 第 30 页 · Figma 帧 30 — A Modular Human–Ant Co-creation System

**EN**  My project explores how people and ants can create a space together. It combines a tabletop system with a digital environment to explore everyday stress relief and attention restoration. I’ll start with an overview of the thesis.

**中文**  我的项目探索人和蚂蚁如何共同创造空间，将桌面系统与数字环境结合起来，探讨对日常压力缓解和注意力恢复的支持。接下来，我先概述论文的整体结构。

## 第 31 页 · Figma 帧 31 — Thesis structure and research logic

**EN**  This map brings the research and design together. I begin with digital pressure and the research question. Slow Technology and Attention Restoration Theory suggest value in waiting and less effortful attention. Research on participation across species and virtual bodies informs co-creation and scale. The midterm installation reveals a timing mismatch, leading to a tabletop system. I then explain three connected design directions: Slow Down, Shift Perspective, and Reconnect. They work together within one experience. The studies guide these choices, but do not prove this project’s effects. I finish with the limits and contribution.

**中文**  这张图把研究依据和设计过程联系起来。我先从数字交互压力和研究问题出发。Slow Technology 与注意力恢复理论提示了等待和较少认知努力的注意方式的价值；跨物种参与和虚拟身体研究则为共创与尺度提供依据。中期装置暴露出时间差问题，推动项目转向桌面系统。随后，我会展开三个相互联系的设计方向：放慢节奏、转换视角和重新连接。它们共同构成完整体验。已有研究引导了这些选择，但不等于证明本项目的效果。最后，我会讨论局限与贡献。

## 第 32 页 · Figma 帧 32 — Research background and question

**EN**  The starting point is the pressure to keep responding to messages, tasks, and notifications. Research on interruptions links faster work with greater stress. My main users are urban young adults with high everyday pressure. I ask whether a longer human–ant co-creation process could help them step away from constant response and focus on a slower natural process.

**中文**  研究的出发点是不断回应消息、任务和通知的压力。关于工作中断的研究将更快的工作节奏与更大压力联系起来。我的主要用户是日常压力较高的城市年轻成年人。我想知道，较长时间展开的人–蚂蚁共创，能否让人暂时离开持续回应的状态，关注一种缓慢的自然过程。

## 第 33 页 · Figma 帧 34 — The full-scale installation

**EN**  The project first took the form of a full-scale installation. A camera tracked people moving across a hexagonal projection. Their position changed the light in a matching Digging Unit. The intended feedback also went the other way: ant tunnel paths returned to the floor projection and could affect people's next movements. The system therefore linked human movement and ant activity through a shared environment, rather than direct commands.

**中文**  项目最初是一个全尺度互动装置。摄像头识别参与者在六边形投影区域中的移动，并改变对应挖掘活动模块中的灯光。反馈也向相反方向发生：蚂蚁形成的洞穴路径返回地面投影，进一步影响人的移动。系统通过一个共同环境将人和蚂蚁的活动联系起来，而不是通过直接命令进行控制。

## 第 34 页 · Figma 帧 35 — Temporal mismatch and design iteration

**EN**  The installation revealed a timing problem. Human movement and light feedback were quick, but tunnel formation was slow. During a short visit, people could see light changes without seeing new paths. I began to treat that delay as a design resource. Photobox offered a useful comparison through waiting, anticipation, and repeated visits. The project moved towards a tabletop system, where people could care for ants and observe changes over several days.

**中文**  这个装置暴露出时间差问题。人的移动和灯光反馈很快，洞穴形成却很慢。在短暂参观中，人能看到灯光变化，却看不到新路径。因此，我开始将延迟视为设计资源。Photobox 中的等待、期待与反复返回提供了参考。项目由此转向桌面系统，让人能够在数天内照料蚂蚁并观察变化。

## 第 35 页 · Figma 帧 36 — Slow Down

**EN**  In Slow Down, people assemble the modules and return from time to time. Some visits reveal a new path, while others show almost no change. A Feeding Unit supports basic care, while a Digging Unit holds the soil and tunnel. The experience grows through small observations, with waiting becoming a pattern of returning and noticing.

**中文**  在放慢节奏这一部分，使用者搭建模块，并间歇地返回观察。有时会发现新路径，有时几乎看不到变化。供养模块支持基本照料，挖掘活动模块容纳土层与洞穴。体验在细微观察中逐渐形成，等待也成为反复返回与逐渐发现的过程。

## 第 36 页 · Figma 帧 37 — Human–ant co-creation and illumination

**EN**  The person sets conditions, but the ants form the tunnel. Area-based coloured illumination is one way to influence the local environment. Research on ant vision supports sensitivity to different parts of the light spectrum, but does not give us precise control over paths. Formica Forma is a useful precedent for light intervention. Its targeted light differs from the broader illumination proposed here.

**中文**  人设置环境条件，蚂蚁则形成洞穴。彩色面光是影响局部环境的一种方式。蚂蚁视觉研究支持其对不同光谱的敏感性，但并不提供精确控制路径的方法。Formica Forma 是一个光照介入的相关案例，它的定点光照与本项目较大范围的面光不同。

## 第 37 页 · Figma 帧 38 — Shift Perspective

**EN**  The paths in a phone image become the basis for a digital environment. In the updated version, this is a ground-level landscape, not an enclosed tunnel. Users explore at an ant-like scale and can switch between first-person and third-person views. They move from looking down at the paths to moving along them. Research on virtual bodies informs this change in scale. The first-person shift of scale remains a key idea.

**中文**  手机照片中的平面路径是数字环境的基础。新版不再把它做成封闭洞穴，而是让使用者在接近地面的蚂蚁尺度景观中探索，并在第一、第三人称之间切换。使用者由俯视路径转为沿着路径移动。虚拟身体研究为尺度与感知的关系提供理论参考；这里延续的重点仍是第一人称尺度转换。

## 第 38 页 · Figma 帧 39 — Personalised editing

**EN**  Personalised editing lets people continue creating. They can choose ant avatars and environmental assets, such as fungal forms and surrounding vegetation. They keep the paths and branches made by the ants, but change the surrounding atmosphere. These images show different settings. The ants provide the structure, while people shape how that structure is experienced.

**中文**  个人化编辑让使用者继续创造。他们可以选择蚂蚁角色和环境素材，例如真菌形态与周围植被，在保留蚂蚁形成的路径与分支的同时，改变环境氛围。这些图片展示了不同的设定。蚂蚁提供结构，而人的选择决定这个结构以怎样的环境被体验。

## 第 39 页 · Figma 帧 40 — Reconnect

**EN**  These maps show how hexagonal spaces connect and how users choose where to go. The wider proposal lets people explore another person’s environment online and notice their choices through its paths and visual settings. They do not need to reply immediately. This is low-pressure digital connection. The prototype shows map-based access; the broader cloud network remains a direction for development.

**中文**  这些地图展示了不同的六边形空间如何连接，以及使用者如何选择要进入的位置。更完整的设计设想，是让人在线探索他人的环境，通过路径与视觉选择感知对方，而不必立即回应。这就是低压力数字连接。现在的原型展示了地图访问，更广泛的云端连接仍是后续发展方向。

## 第 40 页 · Figma 帧 41 — Conclusion

**EN**  To conclude, the project brings slow interaction, a first-person shift of scale, and low-pressure digital connection into one experience. Its key contribution is treating biological delay as a design resource. Waiting and microscopic observation offer an exploratory path for everyday stress relief and attention restoration.

**中文**  最后，项目将慢交互、第一人称尺度转换和低压力数字连接组织为一段完整体验。其核心贡献，是把生物节奏带来的延迟作为设计资源，让等待与微观观察为日常压力缓解和注意力恢复提供一条探索性的路径。
